# Do the 2016 classifiers make the same mistakes?

If three classifiers err on different frames, a majority vote corrects most of
their errors. If they err on the same frames, voting cannot help. This page
measures which case holds for the best three models of the literature
comparison on RML2016.10a: ICRNNA, LSTM2 and MCLDNN.

**Setup.** No new training. The checkpoints come from `train_backbone.py
--arch`, and all three share the same frames, split and seeds. For each seed,
`src/vote_fusion.py` rebuilds the test set (33,000 frames) and runs every model
on it. It refuses to continue unless each model's per-SNR confusion matrices
match its stored ones exactly, and they do. Pooled over 3 seeds that is
**99,000 frames**: 4,950 per SNR label and 24,750 at labels ≥ 10 dB. Counts are
in `vote_rml2016_ICRNNA_LSTM2_MCLDNN.json`. `tools/vote_analysis.py` prints
every number below.

## Result

| | all SNR labels | ≥ 10 dB |
|---|---|---|
| all three agree | 77.0% | 94.4% |
| two against one | 18.4% | 5.1% |
| all three differ | 4.6% | 0.5% |
| ICRNNA alone | 62.23% | 91.48% |
| majority vote (ICRNNA when all three differ) | **62.90%** | **92.34%** |
| averaged softmax | 63.13% | 92.31% |
| at least one of the three right | 68.43% | 93.56% |

- **The vote helps a little, and reliably.**
  - Overall it gains 0.67 points over ICRNNA. The gain appears in each of the
    three seeds: +0.57, +0.71 and +0.72.
  - Against ICRNNA it corrects 1,916 frames and spoils 1,256.
  - At ≥ 10 dB it gains 0.86 points (263 corrected, 51 spoiled).
- **When all three differ** (4,573 frames), one of them is right on 2,349, about
  half.
- **The last row is the ceiling.** No rule that picks one of the three answers
  can beat it, and at ≥ 10 dB it is 93.6%.

## Why the gain is small: the errors are shared

|  | all SNR labels | ≥ 10 dB |
|---|---|---|
| all three wrong, measured | 31.6% | 6.4% |
| all three wrong, if errors were independent | 5.6% | 0.06% |
| two of three right, if errors were independent | 67.2% | 97.9% |
| majority vote, measured | 62.9% | 92.3% |

- **The models are wrong on the same frames.** The correlation between any two
  models' error indicators is 0.76–0.82.
- **At ≥ 10 dB the shared errors are one cell.** On 1,508 frames all three give
  the same wrong answer. That is 68–72% of each model's errors, and 1,331 of
  those 1,508 (88%) are WBFM read as AM-DSB.
- **With independent errors, a vote of three 91.5% classifiers would reach
  about 98%.** These reach 92.3%.

What the vote changes at ≥ 10 dB, by class (2,250 frames per class):

| class | ICRNNA wrong | vote wrong | corrected | spoiled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WBFM | 1,358 | 1,353 | 7 | 2 |
| AM-SSB | 236 | 171 | 74 | 9 |
| QAM64 | 208 | 141 | 82 | 15 |
| QAM16 | 184 | 118 | 83 | 17 |
| 8PSK | 38 | 31 | 9 | 2 |
| all other classes | 85 | 83 | 8 | 6 |

**Two kinds of error, and the vote separates them.**

- **WBFM → AM-DSB is the same in every model**, so it belongs to the data. The
  usual explanation is silence in the source audio, not tested here. The vote
  does nothing for it.
- **The QAM16 ↔ QAM64 errors mostly fall on different frames in each model**:
  only 40 of them are shared by all three. The vote removes about a third of
  them, and the AM-SSB errors behave the same way.

## Five models

Adding PET-CGDNN and VT-CNN2 (`vote_rml2016_ICRNNA_LSTM2_MCLDNN_PETCGDNN_VTCNN2.json`)
changes nothing:

- **Majority of five:** 62.89% overall and 92.34% at ≥ 10 dB, the same as the
  majority of three.
- **Averaged softmax:** 63.18% and 92.48%.

Most of what the five-way vote corrects belongs to the weakest model: 8,855 of
VT-CNN2's frames, against 2,144 of ICRNNA's.

## What it means

These classifiers are close to one classifier trained three times.

- **Different architectures still learn the same decision.** That decision
  fails on the frames the data makes ambiguous.
- **Combining them buys under one point.** No combination can exceed the ceiling
  in the Result table: 68.4% overall and 93.6% at ≥ 10 dB.
- **The binary version comes next.** Fuse each classifier's decision using its
  detection and false-alarm probabilities.
  - The optimal rule for that problem assumes the local decisions are
    independent given the class.
  - An error correlation of 0.8 says to expect the same small gain there. The
    binary case is the place to measure how far the correlation erodes the
    rule's gain.
